# Section 8 — Loyalty Psychology (The Game Layer)

> Turn PLAYPLATE into a game worth playing for years — feeding the deep motivators
> (autonomy, competence, relatedness, 1.13) — while refusing every dark pattern that makes
> loyalty programs feel like traps. This is the mechanism behind the **REPEAT** in
> **EAT • PLAY • REPEAT**.

---

## 8.1 Design principles (the anti-dark-pattern charter) 🔒

1. **Attainable & fair** — a casual guest makes real progress; rewards aren't hoarded for
   whales. (1.14 fairness audit 📊.)
2. **Value real, honest, and honoured** — rewards are genuinely worth it; never a
   bait-and-switch; never quietly devalued.
3. **Respect life** — a holiday or a busy month doesn't nuke a year of loyalty; forgiving
   streaks, generous grace periods.
4. **Fun, not grind** — meaning and mastery over hollow point-farming (1.13).
5. **No predatory mechanics** — **no real-money loot boxes/gambling**, no pay-to-win, no
   manufactured expiry pressure, no compulsion loops (1.11). Optional spend/time limits,
   especially for younger guests.
6. **Belonging over extraction** — the endgame is community and identity, not maximised
   spend.

> The loyalty program is measured by **satisfaction and healthy repeat visits**, never by
> compulsive engagement or spend-until-it-hurts. A guest who feels the program *respects*
> them is the win.

## 8.3 The reward economy (blended schedule) 🧠

Following reward-schedule science (1.14), the economy blends:

- **Predictable core** — coins per spend/play, clear tier benefits. Reliable, trust-
  building. (Fixed-ratio.)
- **Rhythmic missions** — daily/weekly/monthly goals that build the visit habit (1.10).
  (Fixed-interval.)
- **Occasional delight** — surprise drops, mystery rewards, "spin" moments — *modest, real,
  and non-gambling*, tuned for joy not compulsion (1.11). (Variable-ratio, capped & ethical.)
- **Status & mastery** — badges, ranks, achievements that feed *intrinsic* pride and don't
  crowd out the love of the activity (over-justification guard, 1.14).

**Coin economics** are modelled and monitored (breakage, inflation, margin impact) by the
engine so the program is *sustainable* and *fair* — generous enough to delight, disciplined
enough to fund itself (dashboards, Section 9).

## 8.4 Levels & tiers (the progression spine)

A sample ladder (tunable):

```
 Rookie ──▶ Player ──▶ Pro ──▶ Legend ──▶ Creator (community/creator tier)
   │          │          │        │            └─ events access, creator perks, co-marketing
   │          │          │        └─ premium perks, priority booking, signature surprises
   │          │          └─ better earn rate, exclusive missions, early access
   │          └─ personalised offers unlock, squad features
   └─ welcome reward, basic earn, first missions
```

Tier design rules 🔒: benefits are **additive delights**, never gates on *basic* good
service; maintenance is **generous and transparent**; a lapse triggers *care and win-back*
(1.9), not a punitive purge.

## 8.5 Missions & seasons (the living narrative)

- **Daily** — tiny, achievable, habit-forming ("order your favourite," "play with a
  friend").
- **Weekly** — a bit more effort, more reward, some variety ("try 2 new games," "bring the
  squad").
- **Monthly / seasonal** — themed, aspirational, story-driven ("Summer Championship,"
  "Creator Season"), tying into events and tournaments for anticipation and "what's next"
  (1.29 entertainment psychology). Seasons keep the game fresh and give lapsed guests a
  natural re-entry point.

Missions are **personalised** by the engine (a gamer gets game missions, a foodie gets
menu-discovery missions) and **balanced** so they encourage healthy variety, not
overspend.

---

## 8.6 Social, referrals & community (the moat)

- **Squads** — persistent friend groups with shared goals, co-op missions, and squad
  rankings (relatedness + investment, 1.12/1.10). The single strongest retention mechanic.
- **Referrals** — both referrer and friend benefit (reciprocity, honest value). Referral
  quality (do referred guests stay?) is tracked, not just volume.
- **Community rankings** — multiple ladders (casual & serious) so no one is perpetually at
  the bottom (1.21); seasonal resets give everyone a fresh shot.
- **Creator community** — the aspirational top: creators get status, perks, events, and
  co-marketing, turning top guests into brand engines (brand affinity, Section 2).

Community is where PLAYPLATE's loyalty becomes *identity* — the deepest, least-copyable
moat (oxytocin/belonging, 1.12).

---

## 8.7 Personalisation of the game layer 🧠

The engine tailors missions, offers, and challenges to each guest's behaviour and the
business's needs (e.g. off-peak-fill missions for flexible guests, Section 12) — always
within the fairness and anti-dark-pattern charter (8.1). It also *paces* challenge to keep
guests in flow (1.21): not so hard they quit, not so easy they're bored.

---

## 8.8 Responsible-play & spend safeguards 🔒

Because this layer combines gaming + rewards + spend, it carries the strongest safeguards:

- **No real-money gambling mechanics.** Rewards are earned through experience, not bought as
  gambles. No paid loot boxes.
- **Optional self-set spend & time limits**, surfaced proactively, defaulted stricter for
  younger guests (1.18).
- **"Take a break" nudges** on long sessions; healthy-play messaging.
- **Impulse guard** (2.2) reduces pressure on high-impulse guests.
- **Minor protections** (Section 11) apply the strictest settings by default.

---

## 8.9 Measuring loyalty success 📊

Primary: **repeat-visit rate, retention curve, tier progression, squad formation, referral
*quality*, community participation, and self-reported satisfaction with the program.**
Guarded: spend metrics are tracked but a spend rise accompanied by falling satisfaction or
rising responsible-play flags is treated as a **problem**, not a win (delivery/kpis.md).
