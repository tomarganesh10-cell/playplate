# Section 4 — Employee Psychology AI (Wellbeing, Growth & Fairness)

> The most sensitive system in this bible. It exists to make PLAYPLATE the **best place to
> work** in the category — fair schedules, real growth, early support before burnout — with
> the employee's *own voice* at the centre and a **hard rule** that no AI output is ever the
> sole basis for a disciplinary or livelihood decision.

---

## 4.1 The employee-AI constitution (non-negotiable) 🔒

1. **Assistive, never authoritative.** The system *supports* managers and *empowers*
   employees. It **never** automatically disciplines, penalises, ranks-for-firing, or
   decides pay. A human manager decides; the manager is accountable; the reasoning is
   recorded.
2. **Consent, voice & transparency.** Employees know exactly what is measured, why, and can
   see their own data. Wellbeing signals are **self-reported and voluntary**; nothing covert.
3. **Wellbeing first.** The primary purpose is *support* — spot burnout, fatigue, and unfair
   load early and *help*, not to squeeze more output.
4. **No covert surveillance.** No secret audio/video scoring, no keystroke spying, no
   bathroom-break timing, no productivity-panopticon. Measured signals are operational
   (attendance, completed training) or *self-reported* (mood check-in) or *consented and
   contextual* (customer feedback about a service moment).
5. **Right to challenge.** Any employee can question, contextualise, or appeal any
   AI-surfaced signal, and a human must genuinely consider it.
6. **Collective fairness.** The system is audited so it never systematically disadvantages
   any group (fairness testing, Section 11), and it is designed *with* employees, not
   imposed on them.

> This constitution governs every field and feature below. Where a capability and this
> constitution conflict, the constitution wins.

## 4.2 What is measured — and how (the honest table)

| Dimension | Source (how) | Nature | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mood** | *Voluntary* shift check-in (emoji/1-tap, optional note) | Self-reported | Support offer, trend for the employee's own view |
| **Stress** | Self-report + *workload* signals (hours, back-to-back peak shifts, understaffing) | Self + operational | Break/rest nudge, staffing fix, manager check-in |
| **Motivation** | Self-report + participation in growth/recognition | Self + behavioural | Coaching, recognition, new challenges |
| **Burnout risk** | Composite: hours worked, consecutive shifts, declined breaks, self-reported energy, PTO gaps | Blended (transparent) | **Early support**: rest, reschedule, manager conversation |
| **Customer interaction quality** | *Aggregated, consented* guest feedback tied to service moments | External feedback | Coaching, recognition — **contextualised**, never a lone verdict |
| **Communication** | Peer/manager feedback, coordination signals | Human + behavioural | Training, team-fit support |
| **Attendance** | Roster/clock system | Operational | Fair scheduling; flags *patterns to discuss*, not auto-penalties |
| **Learning progress** | LMS completion, certifications | Operational | Next module, unlock roles, growth path |
| **Leadership potential** | Initiative, mentoring, reliability, peer trust | Blended | Development track, shift-lead opportunities |
| **Conflict detection** | Reported incidents, feedback patterns | Human-reported primarily | Early HR mediation, support — **never** auto-blame |
| **Training needs** | Skill gaps vs role, feedback themes | Blended | Targeted, supportive training |
| **Performance** | Balanced scorecard (quality, growth, reliability, teamwork, guest impact) | Blended | Recognition, development, *manager-led* review |

> **On "customer interaction quality" and "performance."** These are the fields most open to
> abuse. Guardrails: they are **inputs to a human manager's holistic judgement**, always
> shown *with context* (Was the venue understaffed? Was a guest abusive? Is the sample tiny?),
> never a single automated score that decides someone's fate. A dip triggers a *supportive*
> conversation and coaching first — not a penalty.

## 4.3 What the AI is allowed to *suggest* (assistive outputs)

Per your brief — all as **suggestions to a human**, all overridable, all logged:

- **Shift changes** — flag likely burnout/fatigue or unfair load; propose fairer rosters
  (balanced peak/off-peak, honoured preferences, adequate rest between shifts).
- **Training** — recommend the next module or a targeted skill based on role and (kind,
  contextual) feedback.
- **Rewards & recognition** — surface people who deserve praise *that might otherwise be
  missed* (the quiet high performer, the person who covered a tough shift). Recognition is
  one of the highest-ROI wellbeing levers (1.12).
- **Breaks** — nudge rest on long/intense shifts; protect breaks from being skipped.
- **Coaching** — suggest coaching topics and pair mentors with mentees.
- **Recognition rituals** — feed team celebrations, milestones, and growth moments.

**It does not suggest:** who to fire, who to demote, or how to cut pay. Those are human
decisions, made through HR process, informed by *many* inputs, never by this system alone.

## 4.4 The burnout early-warning system (worked design)

Burnout is expensive (turnover, poor guest experience) and, above all, *harmful to people*.
The engine treats it as the flagship employee-AI use case:

```
 SIGNALS (self + operational)                RISK MODEL 🧠            SUPPORTIVE ACTION
 ─ self-reported energy/mood trend  ─┐
 ─ hours & consecutive shifts        ├──▶  burnout_risk (0–1,   ──▶  Tiered, HUMAN-led:
 ─ declined/skipped breaks           │      with explanation)         low: gentle self-view + rest nudge
 ─ PTO not taken                     │                                med: manager check-in offered
 ─ peak-shift density                ─┘                                high: proactive schedule relief,
                                                                            wellbeing conversation, PTO
```

Key design choices:
- The employee sees their *own* risk and its drivers **first** — it's a tool for *them*.
- High risk routes to **relief and care**, never to "you're underperforming."
- Managers are trained (SOPs, `delivery/sops.md`) to treat a high-risk flag as *"how can we
  help?"*, and the system measures whether managers *actually* provide relief (manager
  accountability, Section 9 HR dashboard).

## 4.5 Fairness in scheduling (a concrete win)

Poor scheduling is the #1 driver of hospitality churn. The engine optimises rosters for:
- **Fairness** — equitable distribution of desirable/undesirable shifts.
- **Preference honouring** — availability and stated preferences respected as far as
  demand allows, with transparency when they can't be.
- **Adequate rest** — no "clopening," minimum rest between shifts, humane consecutive-shift
  limits.
- **Predictability** — schedules published early; last-minute changes minimised and
  compensated.
- **Demand match** — staff-to-forecast alignment (Section 12) so no one is drowning
  understaffed or sent home with no hours.

The optimiser proposes; a manager approves; employees can swap/request within fair rules.
Fairness metrics are tracked and visible (Section 9).

## 4.6 Growth & recognition (the retention engine)

- **Visible growth path** — skills → certifications → roles → leadership, gamified in a
  *healthy*, motivating way (competence + autonomy, 1.13).
- **Recognition surfacing** — the engine ensures good work is *seen*, especially the quiet
  kind, countering the manager bias toward only-the-loudest.
- **Peer recognition** — lightweight kudos that feed team belonging (1.12, mirror neurons
  1.16 — happy teams create happy guests).
- **Learning-in-flow** — micro-training nudged at teachable moments, not dumped as
  homework.

---

## 4.7 Privacy & governance for employee data 🔒

- **Data minimisation** — collect only what serves wellbeing, fairness, and growth.
- **Access control** — an employee's self-reported wellbeing detail is *theirs*; managers
  see what's needed to *support* (e.g. "burnout risk high, offer relief"), not raw private
  notes, unless the employee shares them.
- **No secret profiles** — employees can see their data and the model's reasoning.
- **Retention limits** — wellbeing/mood data has short retention; growth records persist as
  the employee's own portfolio.
- **Works-council / labour-law alignment** — deployed in consultation with employees and in
  compliance with local employment and data-protection law (Section 11).
- **Auditability** — every suggestion and every human decision it informed is logged for
  fairness auditing and appeal.

---

## 4.8 The line, restated 🔒

> **Never use AI as the sole basis for a disciplinary or livelihood decision.**

This is written into the system as a *technical and process control*: employee-AI outputs
are labelled "assistive — human decision required," disciplinary workflows *require* a
documented human rationale that stands on its own, and the system refuses to emit a
"recommend termination/penalty" output at all. The engine's job with employees is to
**help them thrive** — and, not coincidentally, thriving employees are the single biggest
driver of the guest happiness everything else in this bible chases.
